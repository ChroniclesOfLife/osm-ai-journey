Scene 03 · Trevi Fountain

The roar of falling water swallows the city noise as you step into the little piazza. Neptune presides over the marble, his sea-horses frozen mid-plunge, and the evening crowd presses close, coins glinting in their palms.

Ask the old vendor whether the coin tradition is really true.

The vendor laughs, a dry sound under his grey moustache. “True? Signore, I have sold postcards here forty years — and every face that tosses a coin, I see again.” He presses a warm bronze coin into your palm without being asked. “One for the return. Two for love. Three…” he shrugs, “three is for the brave.”

Toss one coin over my shoulder and make the wish I came here with.

You turn your back to Neptune, the way the old rite demands. The coin leaves your fingers, catches a last blade of evening light, and vanishes into the turquoise with a sound too small to hear over the fountain. The vendor nods once, satisfied, as if a contract has been signed.
